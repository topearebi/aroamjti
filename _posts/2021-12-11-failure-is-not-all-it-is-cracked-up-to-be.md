From the moment we are born into this world, multiple life paths open to us. Depending on our choices, these paths split, intersect, diverge and evolve in ways that we cannot very often foresee. They, however, lead to generally two classes of destinations; success, or failure. You can often tell where the path you’re on is heading towards.

In the earlier stages of my life, it felt like I was on the first of these paths. From private high school to federal university, despite the complexities involved, I somehow scaled through. I have never been the sharpest tack in the room but if you push hard enough, I could break skin. There have been flashes of brilliance here and there. A few spelling bees. Almost every English exam. And yet when the true class geniuses were getting ‘best in subject’ awards, I got awarded for being the neatest.

There is something to be said for being so clean that your learning institution takes notice of it I suppose, but perhaps even then, during this most formative period of my development, my sense of failure was already growing.

As a child, I would get socially anxious. If forced to partake in any public social interaction I would get these hot flashes and begin to sweat profusely. Over time, this general fear of people became the more acceptable particular fear of speaking in public. This fear persists in the present day. I find myself struggling to connect with strangers or to engage in conversation, particularly if I have to initiate them. I still get hot flashes. Worst of all perhaps, is the fact that I cannot dance. I do not mean that I dance badly or that I look ridiculous when I do it. I mean that I simply cannot engage in the act of dancing. It is impossible for me to move my body in accordance with a set rhythm.

No one tells you how important being able to dance is to have a functional social life. Most flirting usually cumulates in a dance or two. if you’re looking to have wild spontaneous sex it is almost a rite of passage. Mating dances are not restricted to the lower animals. To keep friendships alive, a party or two is required. So you can either settle for fleeting friendships or go to parties where everyone asks you why you’re not having fun as you struggle to fend off a panic attack at every scream and drumbeat and “why are you just sitting there come dance with me”.

When you are faced with a problem, you can either find a solution or a workaround. I.e solve the problem or find a way to bypass it. Problem-solving is a skill that requires objectivity which can be elusive when we are diagnosing personal issues. In childhood development, one must graduate from the stage of awareness of oneself to awareness of others, and then to awareness of the world. I seem to have passed through these stages without managing to integrate them as connected experiences. I would later come to learn, through various unreliable online psychological tests, that I am some part autistic, some part bipolar, some part afflicted with social anxiety disorder. Basically a nutcase. It is in this frame of mind that I stumbled upon the answer to my problem. You cannot, in fact, suck at socialization if you do attempt to socialize.

Perhaps my current condition is due to the silo-like conditions in which I was raised. Growing up, I had very limited social interaction with anybody, including my parents. All I managed to form in terms of social capabilities seems to be a codependent need for constant presence. I do not understand the world, I do not understand people. And I do not understand my place in it all. I am constantly at a loss of what to do in social contexts. This uncertainty of how to live, and what next to do feels like I am failing at life.

In spite of myself, I have had flashes of something. Not brilliance, but flashes nonetheless. Having developed some sense of perspective over the years, I am now looking for solutions instead of workarounds. I am slowly but surely facing my failure head-on. 

One recurring piece of advice that every guru and youtube life hacker recommends is gratitude. When you feel like a failure, learn to count your blessings and you will realize that you do not have it so hard after all. I have two degrees, and while I feel no sense of pride about it, most people do not manage to get one as I have come to find out. I am in a relationship which has, shall we say, gone the distance? A quick doomscrolling session will tell you that the chances of that are not exactly the highest in this age. I have six dogs who seem to love me somewhat. The only problem is, that these things, while they may seem fine, are way less than I envisioned when I first dreamt them. And sadly, I do not think I will ever dance
