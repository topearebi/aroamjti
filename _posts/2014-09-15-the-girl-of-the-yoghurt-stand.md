I'm terrible with introductions. I never know how to begin. Its rather hard to decide whether to just get right to the point, or start off with some sort of preamble. Guess I chose the latter as we can well see.

So, back to the girl of the yoghurt stand. It was a warm summer's day...

Okay, so we don't have summer in Nigeria, but it was worth a shot. I mean, classic beginning.

Well, I was coming home from school one day, I had taken a bus half way and was walking to the next bus-stop., when I noticed this girl in front me. She wasn't tall, but not so terribly short. Just the right height (i.e, tall enough to fit right on my chest when hugging her). Not skinny, not fat, but with enough meat flesh as to be comfortable when holding her. In short, she was hot

Well, I had nothing better to do, so I followed her. I mean, she was walking the same way I was anyway. Seemed like she had gotten out of her house just to come get something in the market. How lucky for me.

She walked on and on. Past many stalls, and I just followed. I mean, it was obvious she was leading me on. In my head, I went through the many possible scenarios I could use to strike up a conversation with her and maybe if I'm lucky, get her number. Though the notion of stopping a person in the middle of a market just to make mindless chit-chat did strike me as rather absurd. (Kudos to the guys who do this on a daily basis cos I don't have the liver). Then it happened. It had been on my mind for a while now - the thirst - and I had been on the lookout for a yoghurt stand.  I wanted to buy superyogo. Then just as I had given up the whole idea of acquiring a new acquaintance, she also topped to buy yoghurt, just like me. It was then I started to think;

This must be fate. I mean, what else could it be. Like what are the odds that the female person I've been stalking for several feet now stops for a break at the same spot I stop. After she makes her purchase (about 3 superyogos or so). She has some problem with the change, I calculate it for her then strike up a conversation from there. She ends up giving me her number. Easy. At least that how it went in my head.

Reality, as we all know, hardly ever matches imagination. As she was telling the yoghurt vendor what she wanted to buy, I couldn't help but just stare and wonder. In those few moments, I fell in love, and loved for a lifetime. I grew old and sacrificed for love. in just those few moments. I lived, and died.

An argument brought me back from my reverie. It was the girl, she was arguing with the vendor on how much her change was. My heartbeat suddenly spiked. Was it coming it true? The perfect life I had just imagined a few moments, was staring me right in the face. As quick as my mathematically challenged brain would allow me, which wasn't so long, I calculated the total price and the change she was supposed to collect and resolved the issue. Then she spoke to me.

She said, "thank you. I'm going to trust that you go it right" or words to that effect (the memory is faint as I was too focused on her voice to understand her words). While I gazed on, frozen in my own stupor at the wonder of what had just hapnd, the first step in my dream plan had been fulfilled, she left. Just like that she was gone. I never even got to know her name. All I got, were the memories. Memories of a life that I never got to live. She gave me that much and I'm grateful. I love you very much, Girl of The Yoghurt Stand.
