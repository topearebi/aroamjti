Yesterday I listened to how Amazon once planned for almost 2 years before shopping a product and how they believe this is the only way to build anything truly valuable. To plan and research extensively beforehand. As a person who is very critical of my ability to execute, this is something I have internalised to some degree as well. My favourite app is Google Sheets. Next is Google Docs.

It has always seemed ridiculous to me to commit to action without at least understanding all the variables, even if they cannot be determined.

This process is rooted in my childhood where I would often make lists of things. I had books dedicated to making infinite lists so I could understand my thoughts. Even today, my favourite prompt for LLMs is "outline".

And yet, I am not wrong about my failures of execution. Even the most well-thought-out process means nothing if it is not acted on. Well, unless it's automated, but that's another thought vomit.

My attempts at self-diagnostics tell me that I've either spent such a long time operating this way that my brain chemistry has decided follow through is not rewarding enough, or that the consequences of failure are simply not threatening enough to warrant follow though. 

I'm more inclined to the latter seeing as I do seem to always make academic and work-related deadlines, it's the big life projects that I seem to fail at. Also, I suppose I still deep down somewhere believe, as I did in my angsty teen phase, that all consequences are not as significant as the ultimate consequence of life - death. And it is inevitable.

This is an interesting concept because more often people seem to relate immortality to a loss of meaning, and yet I seem to think life only has value if it never ends.

Why work on something if your deadline could be right this moment.

Yet, it would be a shame to not see all of my dreams come to fruition. They're pretty good ones. Not just for me, but for everyone.
