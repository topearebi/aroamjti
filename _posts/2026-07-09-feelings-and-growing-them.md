I always have something to write about. Whether it's how my day went, specific things that happened during it or how I felt about them. The feelings they evoked. The resulting memories and connections my brain made as it processed them. But I don't always write about them. In fact I rarely ever do as writing is an action that requires more reflection than doing. It is not an automatic activity that you can offload to your subconscious while you press the exact same buttons or swipe down a feed over and over again. Each word, each letter, each button press requires thought and planning and consideration. It is the closest thing to the lost of art of writing on paper we'll have for a while.

This morning writing seems to hold the potential to be particularly carthatic.

I've taken a break from dating this whole year. Which, as I write this, I have come to realised meanns I have not kissed anyone outside of my very immediate family in about the same time period. Yesterday I did. I went out with someone and it felt easy. I felt that thrill of excitement. Of possibility. Of thinking this is someone I would enjoy getting to know. Hanging out with. Fighting with. And making up with. 

Of course poly relationships like any relationships can be quite complicated. I have often said that poly relationships are just as difficult to navigate as monogamous ones. But while that may be true emotionally, it can be rather taxing.

One of the ways in which this tax manifests is in the difficulty of actually starting. One of the most common questions when meeting someone new is, how did you and/or your partner know you were poly. This is the first shit test.

Now it is my experience that the answer you do not want to hear back at this stage is, something around it being a recent experience. "I'm trying it out." "I'm not sure I can be monogamous." "My partner and I are trying new things." These answers and their variations fail the shit test. At least with me. I don't date much, but no one ever tries non-monogamy with me and comes out thinking experiment successful. They kind of just always realise that they would prefer to have a more monogamous relationship with me, which I cannot give for obvious reasons. But also and perhaps more significantly, because my answer to the shit test is, "I've always been polyamorous. I've just never had the labels for it that we do now".

I also suspect slightly that it's an energy thing. Not woo-woo spiritual energy. But more the way I put myself into my relationships with people. While I wouldn't exactly call it needy, it can be quite intense. At the same time, I am quite clear that I am not polyamorous to divide my apportion my emotions, my time definitely l, but my emotions? No. My feelings are too big. I am too infinite.

That was quite a dramatic touch.

The other taxing thing about being poly is the size of the pool. For most people, being poly means taking the entire population and cutting it in half first for gender preference. At this point, this is the same total date able population for most cis people. Now you cut that down by maybe 80 percent for people who actively identify as polyamorous. Cut that down again for location. And you find that really, you're seeing the exact same set of people across all the 5 dating apps you now need to meet people, because you cannot walk among the rest of the regular humans chatting people up on the road hoping maybe they're down to hang with you and your wife sometime. Plus at a certain age, that's just creepy.

For me, I don't really have to deal with the gender part because my attraction isn't based on physical traits, but for the same reasons, I do have to cut out the population of people for whom sex is a prime consideration. Which ultimately I think is the point of being poly. The idea that love is limitless, formless, endless. And it truly is, until you need to practice it.
