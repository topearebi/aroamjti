I have a number of projects set out that I'd like to complete. Some short term, from a week to longer term year-spanning ones.

The most important concept that I've come to understand recently and which I think is most critical to internalise is projects and how they necessarily have start dates, and end dates.

Now this although obvious, makes a lot of sense and has become one of the most profound truths that I know hold dear. Let's call it project thinking. If you start something, surely, you should have some sort of idea if the time or general conditions which marke it's end.

At first glance, this seems like a reasonable solution to my problems spelt out in {over Planning. Over Analysis}. The results remain to be seen. Depending on how many projects I am able to complete.

The primary danger to project thinking is the definition of a project. To be bound by time, the project itself must be bound by definition. To be bound by a reasonable or rather, a practical timeframe - practical being a time period within which a reasonable amount of work can be completed, without being so much as to cause a lack of sufficient motivation to completion, or allowing so much uncertainty as to hinder reasonable execution - it must be bound by scope. These conditions introduce an implicit limitation as well, that of the competence of the project executor.

I have had the most fantastic idea for a project today, and that is to create my own problem-solving/decision-making framework. Which sounds both big and small at the same time because decision making is so well studied already that there are tons of tried and tested frameworks, but big as well because there is so much research to cover to begin understanding the problem, and then scaling whatever the optimal framework looks like to fit within the bounds of my competence as project executor.

The idea is that the framework when completed will be applicable to different areas of my life. Gaining the skills for a new job function, learning a new topic or skill, arguing a position.

It could run as a sort of test case through which I could parse my ideas before committing to them.

Of course, the more generalised a solution, the less accurate it may be when applied to specialised cases. It may be that the framework will have different levels or modes of application depending on the use case. Either way, it should be interesting.
